# B2B tech marketing

The market is fragmented — hundreds of small specialized agencies, not a few dominant players. The shape of that market is the answer to most build-vs-buy questions a B2B tech company faces.

## Outsource vs in-house

| Outsource to a specialized agency | Keep in-house |
|---|---|
| Paid ads management (Google, Bing, LinkedIn) | Product positioning and messaging |
| Website development and ongoing maintenance | Core website copy (solution pages, product pages) |
| Long-form SEO content creation (engineer-authored) | Customer case study production |
| Public relations | Brand storyline, naming |
| Marketing operations automation (CRM, workflows, integrations) | Strategic budget allocation across channels |

Two functions are always in-house: defining the company's product positioning, and producing customer case studies. Both rely on internal context no agency has.

## Why agencies must be specialized

Technical B2B marketing is not consumer marketing. A restaurant chain needs local search; a jeweler needs influencers. A Kubernetes tools company needs an author who knows what AWS Cost Explorer, AWS Trusted Advisor, AWS Budgets, OpenCost, Karpenter, and EKS do — well enough to write educational content that ranks and converts. Generic writers who research with AI and copy concepts from other articles produce low time-on-page, which kills rankings.

The best long-form SEO content agencies recruit active engineers who freelance on the side. That pool is small, which is why long-form SEO content is the most natural area to outsource and the hardest area to find capable help in.

## Services taxonomy

| Column | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Planning and messaging** | GTM strategy, account-based marketing, messaging pyramid, technical SEO audit, keyword research |
| **Traffic generation** | Social media (LinkedIn, X), SEO content (long-form), paid ads (Google, Bing, LinkedIn, Reddit, Facebook) |
| **Product promotion** | Product content, case studies, industry events, webinars, PR and backlinks |
| **Tools and KPIs** | CRM (HubSpot, Salesforce), attribution and reporting, marketing automation |
| **Website management** | Core website content, website development and operations, design and graphics |

A company that runs this matrix in-house has at least one specialist per column. A company that outsources runs the matrix with two or three agencies, each owning specific columns — never one agency for everything.

## Long-form SEO content is the primary organic lever

- Short-form content doesn't rank unless competition is very low.
- Long-form articles (1,500–2,000 words minimum) compete on depth. The most comprehensive, unbiased, educational content ranks highest.
- Pillar pages (a cluster of 8–12 long-form articles on a theme) outrank single articles for technical keywords.
- An article reaches its maximum traffic potential after three to four months, holds for a year or two, then decays.
- Website traffic growth depends on the number of new articles published per month, not on ads or social.

The corollary: SEO is a publishing program, not a campaign. Teams that treat content as a quarterly output lose to teams that publish monthly.

## Multi-agency strategy

Do not use one agency for everything even if one offers to. Agency founders have roots in different skills (content, ads, PR, web dev). The right shape is two or three agencies, each owning specific columns of the services taxonomy. Coordination sits in-house with the marketing leader.

## THNKBIG application

THNKBIG runs the SEO column of the matrix with an agent pipeline, not an agency:

- **`skip` / `decay` / `depth`** (detection agents) replace the agency audit-and-brief role.
- **Echo + Editor** (writing + review) replace the freelance-engineer-and-editor role.
- **Vision** (SEO lane owner) replaces the project-manager role.

The strategic frame this skill provides is unchanged: subject-matter expertise is non-substitutable, long-form is the lever, and the build-vs-buy decision favors in-house for product positioning and case studies. THNKBIG keeps case studies in-house (PM + sales + customer success debriefs), positions in-house (Rudy + Pepper), and runs the SEO publishing program in-house via agents.

## Anti-patterns (do not do these)

- Treat this skill as a recommendation to outsource THNKBIG's SEO to an agency. The pipeline is in-house by design.
- Skip the multi-agency caveat because THNKBIG runs one pipeline; the underlying point (subject-matter expertise is non-substitutable) still applies.
- Use the services taxonomy as a checklist to "cover everything"; SEO column is the lever for THNKBIG, not all five columns at once.
- Treat long-form SEO as a campaign; it is a publishing program (monthly cadence, quarterly compounding).
